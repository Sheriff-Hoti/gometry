One loop, every direction

The same two ifs draw steep, reversed, and negative-going lines. Nothing branches on direction — sx/sy carry it.

Recap in one line

On the shallow line from lesson 1, e2 >= dy is true every tick (x is the driving axis) and e2 <= dx fires exactly |dy| = 2 times — one diagonal per unit of height. Now flip the shape.

The mirror: a steep line

Take your test case steep up, (0,0) → (2,5):

dx = 2   dy = -5   sx = 1   sy = 1   err = -3

Watch the simulator. This time e2 <= dx is true every tick — y is the driving axis — and e2 >= dy fires only |dx| = 2 times. Count the diagonal moves: exactly 2, one per unit of width. The rule from lesson 1 didn't change; the axes changed roles (Wikipedia, the generalization).

Direction comes free

Now the reversed horizontal from your tests, (4,3) → (1,3): dx = 3, dy = 0, sx = −1, sy = 0.

(4,3), err=36x += −1 (second if: 6 ≤ 3 is false)(3,3)
(3,3), err=36x += −1(2,3)
(2,3), err=36x += −1(1,3) — stop
The point. Nobody wrote "if going left". sign() decided that once, before the loop, and the loop just obeys sx. Negative coordinates work the same way: the loop never assumes positions are positive — only differences feed dx/dy/sx/sy. That is why your negative coords test passes with zero special-casing.
