Rotation and spin

Two small formulas turn the still life, and a closure you already know keeps it turning.

Rotation is two recipes

Rotating around Y mixes x and z while y sits out (shapes/vec3.go):

x' = x·c + z·s      z' = −x·s + z·c      (c = cos a, s = sin a)

Sanity-check it like your tests do: a quarter turn (a = π/2, so c = 0, s = 1) sends (1, 0, 0) to (0, 0, −1). That is exactly your quarter turn about Y sends +X to -Z test. RotateX is the mirror image mixing y and z (Wikipedia; LearnOpenGL).

Order matters and yours is fixed: Vertices() rotates X first, then Y (code lines 30–31). Different order, different cube — so it is pinned down, not accidental.

Spin is a closure you already own

animations.Spin(0.06, 0.1) returns a function that adds 0.06 to RotX and 0.10 to RotY every 100 ms tick — the same state-in-closure pattern as Bounce and Pendulum, except the state is angles instead of direction. Two details worth noticing:

LineWhy
math.Mod(…, 2π)Angles never grow past one turn, so a week-long run loses no float precision.
nil guardSame house style as every other step function: safe to call on nothing.

At 0.10 rad/tick, a full Y-turn takes ~63 ticks ≈ 6.3 s. Press Play below — it runs the same rates as your app.
